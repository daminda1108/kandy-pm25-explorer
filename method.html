Kandy PM2.5 · 2019–2026

How the valley's air is reconstructed, and how far to trust it.

What the map is made from, in plain words, and which parts of it you can rely on.

This is a research reconstruction of fine-particle air pollution (PM2.5) over the Kandy valley, hour by hour, from 2019 to today, on a 1 km grid. Kandy has only one air-quality monitoring station, so the record is pieced together from satellites, weather data and that station. It is not a measurement, and it is not an official air-quality service.

Where the numbers come from

  • How polluted each year is comes from satellite estimates of fine particles, adjusted so that they agree with the Central Environmental Authority's monitoring station in Kandy.
  • How it changes from hour to hour and day to day comes from weather data and a global air-quality model, combined in a statistical model. The rhythm of the day, with its morning and evening rush-hour peaks and cleaner nights, is matched to the same station.
  • How much of it arrives from outside is estimated separately, so the map can show which part of the pollution comes in with the wind and which part the valley makes itself.
  • Where it sits inside the valley is an assumption: a smooth pattern based on satellite data and the shape of the terrain. It is not measured, which is why the map labels it "indicative".

What you can trust

What the map showsHow sureWhy
The yearly level: how polluted Kandy is on average anchored Adjusted to the Kandy monitoring station using 2019 to mid-2021. Checked on later years that were not used for the adjustment, it agrees to within about 1%. It rests on one station.
The rhythm of the day and the year timing right Rush-hour peaks, the cleaner nights and the months that are worst all match the station. The seasonal swing is too strong, though: in most years the map's worst month is three to four times its cleanest, while at the station it is about twice. A fix is being tested.
Which days are worse indicative Day to day, the map does about as well as a global air-quality forecast adjusted to the station, and no better. Read a single day as a guide, not a reading.
Where in the valley it is worse indicative The map's differences between places are small, a few per cent, and come from an assumption. Real differences in Kandy are much larger but happen street by street, over a few hundred metres, which a 1 km map cannot show. The map cannot tell you which street or town is worse.
How much is made locally and how much arrives from outside estimated About half and half over a year, shifting with the monsoon. This comes from how the model is built rather than from a direct measurement, so treat it as an estimate.

Three kinds of year

  • 2019–2023 are the main record, adjusted to the monitoring station.
  • 2024–2026 are an extension. The satellite record ends in 2023, so the level is modelled from weather instead. Against the station it reads about 40% too low, so treat its level as uncertain.
  • Forecast hours (marked ▸ in the date picker) are a demonstration. Nothing about them has been checked in Kandy, so the range is shown first and deliberately widened.

The range around each number

Every number comes with a 90% range. On the station's later hours, the real value fell inside that range about 87% of the time. The range is wider on haze episodes and in the south-west monsoon, when the map is least sure. The map can also show the top of the range instead of the middle value.

Tested on other cities

Because Kandy has so little monitoring, the same method was run on ten cities that have many monitoring stations, using only the kind of data Kandy has, and then compared with the stations it never saw. In Medellín, Colombia, a tropical valley city, it reproduced the seasons, the daily rhythm and roughly which neighbourhoods are worse.

Health

The map reports concentrations only. It does not estimate illness or deaths: doing that responsibly needs local health data and a settled level, which this reconstruction does not yet have.
