Central Province · Sri Lanka

A city of 400,000 people
with no air quality monitor.

Kandy sits in an enclosed valley about 500 m up, ringed by ridges that trap what the city emits and, for part of the year, hold haze that arrived from halfway across the Indian Ocean. Nobody has ever published a continuous record of its fine particulate pollution. This is an attempt to reconstruct one, and to be precise about which parts of it you can trust.

scroll

01 · The problem

You cannot validate a map of a place with no measurements.

Standard methods learn from a city's own monitors. Kandy has none that are public and continuous, so there is nothing to learn from and, less obviously, nothing to check the answer against. A confident-looking map of an unmonitored city is just a confident -looking map.

02 · The approach

Build the model from physics, then test it where measurements exist.

Every term depends only on data available anywhere on Earth: terrain, reanalysis weather, satellite columns, an open road map. So the identical model can be run for cities that do have dense monitoring networks, deliberately starved of data to match Kandy's situation, and scored against the monitors it never saw.

03 · What the air is made of

About half of Kandy's pollution is not Kandy's.

The field separates a regional background (the haze that arrives over the valley whatever the city does) from the local increment on top of it. The split follows from a physical constraint rather than an assumption: local sources emit continuously, so the background can never equal the total. Imposing that gives roughly a 50/50 split, and it shifts through the year with the monsoon.

04 · Confidence, tiered

Three kinds of year, and they are not equally trustworthy.

2019 – 2023 Level pinned to a satellite product. The years the method was scored on.
2024 – now That satellite anchor ends in 2023, so the level is modelled from weather. Read an hour as indicative of conditions, not as a reading.
Forecast A demonstration. Nothing about it is verified locally, and the interval is deliberately wide.

05 · The honest limit

The street-scale pattern is physics, not measurement.

Where the pollution sits within the valley is imposed from a terrain and traffic model, and its hour-to-hour sharpness has never been calibrated against anything local. Five independent tests say that pattern cannot be learned from public data either. One monitor inside the basin would change that, and it would settle the background question too.
